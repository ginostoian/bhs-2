# Content plan: November 2026 (2 articles a week)

**Goal:** win page-1 rankings for the cost and decision queries that come right before a hire, and feed authority from them into the service pages. Every article is chosen from three inputs:

1. **GSC demand** you already have impressions for (export 27 Jul–26 Oct)
2. **Weak page 1s**: SERPs led by stale (2022–2024) posts, forums or national directories ([serp research](2026-10-serp-research.md))
3. **Commercial proximity**: the reader is months, not years, from hiring a builder

## Rules for every article (what made the #1 renovation cost guide work)

- **One intent per article.** "How much does a loft conversion cost in London" is one article. Planning, types and finance are separate articles that link to each other. No more 8,000-word "complete guides".
- **The number goes in the title.** "Side Return Extension Cost London 2026: £55k–£95k" beats "Side return guide".
- **Answer capsule first:** 40–60 words that directly answer the title question, with a number. This is what AI Overviews and ChatGPT quote.
- **Say where the numbers come from.** "Based on Better Homes quotes for London projects in 2025–26" (pull the ranges from the CRM, below). This is your edge over Checkatrade and the national guides, and the reason journalists and AI tools cite you.
- **H2s are questions people ask.** Use the "People also ask" questions in the briefs as H2s or FAQ entries.
- **1,800–3,000 words.** Enough to be the best answer, short enough to stay on one intent.
- **At least one original asset:** a cost table, a borough comparison, a decision flowchart, or real project photos with a caption (area, scope, timeline). This is what earns links.
- **Internal links:** 1 link to the matching service page (exact service name as anchor), 1 to the matching calculator, 2–3 to sibling articles. Then add a link **to** the new article from the service page's guides block and from 2 older articles.
- **Byline:** Gino, with the role and "12+ years delivering London extensions and renovations". Show "Reviewed by [site lead]" only when someone actually reviewed it.
- **Freshness:** put "Updated [month] 2026" in the visible byline and only change it for a real update.
- **No internal or unfinished-sounding copy.** Every sentence must read as finished, for a homeowner.

### Use your own pricing data (do this first, ~2 hours)
The admin CRM holds quotes and projects. For each project type (rear, side return, wraparound, double storey, dormer loft, hip-to-gable, mansard, full renovation by bedroom count, kitchen, bathroom, basement), pull **median, 25th and 75th percentile quote values and floor areas** for 2025–26. Publish them as "Typical Better Homes quotes, London 2025–26". That one table is the single most linkable and AI-citable asset you can make, and nobody else in the SERPs has it except MVV ("priced on our estimator").

## Distribution (so people actually visit)

For each article, on publish day:
1. **Request indexing** in Search Console (URL Inspection) and add it to the sitemap (automatic via next-sitemap, so check it appears).
2. **Link from the service page** guides block (`RelatedGuides` / `knowledgeCenter.js`) and from 2 older articles.
3. **Google Business Profile post** with the cover image, a 2-line summary and a link (UTM `utm_source=gbp&utm_medium=post`).
4. **Houzz:** post the project photos used in the article as an ideabook / project update linking back.
5. **Instagram / Facebook:** carousel of the cost table (5 slides) plus a link in bio.
6. **Reddit / Mumsnet:** answer an existing live thread on the same question with a genuinely useful answer. Link only if the rules allow and it adds value (Mumsnet ranks #3 for side return costs).
7. **Email:** send to past enquirers who opted in, as a monthly "London renovation costs" note.
8. **Journalist pitch** for articles 3 and 8 (see the link plan).
