**Takeaway:** your pages are **longer, better structured and have better schema** than the pages that outrank them. You're not losing on-page "SEO hygiene". You're losing on four things:

1. **Headings that match searches.** Their H1 and H2s are the phrases people type ("What does a house extension cost in London?", "Types of loft conversion", "Planning permission for loft conversions", "Areas we cover"). Yours were written as brand poetry. Google and AI answer engines use headings to work out what a section answers. A heading like "Feel clear about what happens next" answers nothing.
2. **Authority.** Beams (B Corp, press, years of links), Simply (thousands of lofts, celebrity projects, area-page network) and Plus Rooms (20 years, Dezeen) have far more referring domains than your ~50 backlinks. This is the biggest single gap and can't be fixed on-page.
3. **Offer clarity and proof up front.** Every winner states a concrete promise in the first screen (fixed price, 10-year warranty, free survey, average time) and puts named, located reviews right under it. You do have a 10-year guarantee, 5.0 ratings and 12+ years; they're just hidden or worded abstractly.
4. **Map pack reviews.** Every commercial query shows a 3-pack where winners have **17–405 Google reviews**. The organic page is only half of the first screen.

**Takeaway:** your one #1 page is the one with a **single, clear intent (cost)**. The loft and extension "complete guides" try to answer twenty intents at once, so they rank mid-page 2 for all of them. Competitors that win cost queries put **the number in the title** and say **where the number came from** ("priced on our estimator", "from 40 London jobs").

## Where Better Homes is already ahead

- **Content depth and accuracy.** The renovation cost guide is #1 nationally-flavoured for "house renovation cost london", above Checkatrade and the FMB.
- **Calculators.** You have five (renovation, extension, kitchen, bathroom, BTU). Only Architecture for London and Beams have comparable tools, and theirs rank. Yours have 35k+ impressions waiting for authority and better titles.
- **Structured data.** Your schema graph (Organization → LocalBusiness → Service → FAQ → Breadcrumb) is more complete than any competitor's.
- **AI-answer formatting.** Answer capsules and section IDs are already being quoted (GSC shows section-anchor impressions).
- **Reviews.** 5.0 on Houzz (51 reviews) and Google, plus Best of Houzz 2023. Competitors with fewer reviews advertise theirs harder.

## Copy: is theirs better than ours?

**For ranking and converting a cold searcher, theirs is better. For credibility with a high-budget client who's already reading, ours is better.**

| | Competitors | Better Homes |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | Direct, salesy, benefit-and-offer ("Fixed price. 10-year warranty. Free quote today!") | Calm, premium, emotional ("A home that works beautifully for the life you live.") |
| Specificity | Numbers everywhere: "£68k–£81k", "20 years", "2 mins", "405 reviews" | Numbers mostly hidden in tables lower down; proof framed abstractly |
| Search match | Headings repeat what people type | Headings are invitations, not answers |
| Trust | Named reviewers with neighbourhood under the hero | Strong reviews, but far down the page |
| Risk | Can feel cheap or generic (exclamation marks, all caps) | Can feel vague, hiding *what you do* and *what it costs* |

**What to do:** keep the premium voice, but make every heading answer a question, put one hard number in each hero, and move named, located reviews to the first screen. The fixes on branch `seo/phase-2` already add a search-matching H1 above each emotional line (e.g. "HOUSE EXTENSION BUILDERS IN LONDON / More room, more light…"). The next step is the H2s.

## The single best thing competitors do that we don't

**They build authority off-site and focus it on pages that match search intent exactly.** Beams is the clearest case: thin-ish service pages, no schema, yet it ranks for every service because the domain has years of links, press and B Corp status, and every page is a plain "[service] in London" with a cost section and an FAQ. Simply Loft does the same through a network of `/area/` pages and brand press.

For Better Homes, the gap is **~50 backlinks against an estimated several hundred-plus referring domains for the leaders**, plus **a Google review count too low to win the map pack**. Content is no longer the bottleneck. Links, reviews and search-matching headings are. That's why the roadmap puts most of its weight there.

## Specific gaps to close (priority order)

1. **Search-matching H1s:** done on branch (`seo/phase-2`).
2. **Search-matching H2s on all service pages:** table above.
3. **Hero proof:** add 2–3 named Houzz/Google reviews with area under each service hero, plus "12+ years · 5.0 rating · up to 10-year guarantee".
4. **Single-intent cost pages** for extension, loft, basement, kitchen and bathroom, each with a number in the title. The extension and kitchen ones already exist but need refreshing (2025 → 2026).
5. **A `/prices` hub** like Beams' `/our-prices`: one page with typical London costs for every service, all inc VAT, linking to each cost guide and calculator. It's a natural link-earning asset.
6. **A downloadable guide** (Simply's "Download our guide to extensions") as a lead magnet and reason to link.
7. **Area pages:** rebuild 6–8 core East/North-East London areas (Leytonstone, Walthamstow, Woodford, Wanstead, Hackney, Stoke Newington, Islington, Loughton) around real projects. Create-spaces ranks for "loft conversion companies east london" with a Leytonstone page.
8. **Project pages that rank:** Abbey Extensions and S&M Solutions rank #7–8 with single project pages. Retitle portfolio pages as "Side-return kitchen extension in Forest Gate, E7" instead of a client first name.
9. **Directory presence:** FMB, Checkatrade, TrustMark, MyBuilder (exists), Houzz (exists), TrustATrader, Yell. See the link plan.
10. **Google reviews:** a review-request process at handover. Target 40+ Google reviews within 3 months.
